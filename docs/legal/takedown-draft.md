## 2. Copyright takedowns (DMCA process)

We follow the notice-and-takedown process of the U.S. Digital Millennium
Copyright Act. Registering a designated agent is one of the conditions a U.S.
hosting service must meet to qualify for the DMCA's liability limitation for
user uploads — it is the reason to do the steps below, though operating a
website does not by itself require DMCA registration. **[LAWYER: confirm the
safe-harbor posture for this service.]**

**Our designated agent** (required before launch):
- **[AGENT NAME / TITLE — a role title survives staff changes]**
- **[MAILING ADDRESS — a P.O. box is acceptable for the agent's address]**
- **[EMAIL]**
- **[PHONE]**

The same information must be (a) posted publicly here and (b) registered with
the U.S. Copyright Office's online DMCA Designated Agent Directory
(dmca.copyright.gov, currently a $6 fee per designation), and the designation
must be renewed at least every three years or it expires.

**Sending a notice.** A copyright takedown notice should include:

1. identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed;
2. identification of the material to be removed (the project URL);
3. the complainant's contact information;
4. a statement of good-faith belief that the use is unauthorized;
5. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that the information is accurate and
   the complainant is authorized to act for the copyright owner;
6. a physical or electronic signature.

**Our response.** We remove or disable the material promptly (target: **[TIME
TARGET — suggest 72 hours]**), forward the notice to the uploader, and tell the
complainant what we did.

**Counter-notice.** An uploader may send a counter-notice to the same agent.
Under the statute it must include substantially:

1. the uploader's physical or electronic signature;
2. identification of the removed material and where it appeared before removal;
3. a statement under penalty of perjury that the uploader has a good faith
   belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or
   misidentification of the material to be removed;
4. the uploader's name, address, and telephone number, plus consent to the
   jurisdiction of a U.S. federal district court for any judicial district in
   which the uploader's address is located (or, if the address is outside the
   U.S., any judicial district in which we may be found), and an undertaking to
   accept service of process from the original complainant or their agent.

If we receive a valid counter-notice, we tell the original complainant that we
will replace or restore the material within the statutory window — not less
than 10 and not more than 14 business days after receiving the counter-notice
— unless we first learn the complainant has filed a court action seeking to
restrain the uploader. We are neutral in this: we follow the statute, we do not
judge who is right.

**Repeat infringers.** Accounts with repeated valid notices are closed. We keep
a simple, private log of notices and outcomes to make this enforceable.

## 3. Illegal content, including child-safety reports

Copyright is not the urgent lane. For reports of illegal content:

- **Child sexual abuse material**: remove, and report to the NCMEC
  CyberTipline as soon as reasonably possible after learning of it — every
  time, no discretion. A completed CyberTipline report carries a legal duty to
  preserve the reported material securely for at least one year (with access
  limited to what compliance requires); our account-deletion promises yield to
  that duty. Whether and when to also notify law enforcement directly is a
  separate step the operator takes as appropriate under the circumstances —
  **[LAWYER: confirm the law-enforcement notification practice.]** Operational
  guidance for the operator: do not re-host or forward a copy of the material,
  and do not delay the report for internal review.
- **Other illegal content** (threats, harassment, fraud, IP crimes beyond
  copyright): remove what is reported, keep a record, and report to
  authorities where the law requires or where someone is at risk. **[LAWYER:
  EU DSA "notice and action" mechanics — a hosting service must provide
  notice mechanisms and give statements of reasons for restrictions; small
  and micro enterprises get limited exemptions from some platform duties,
  but not from hosting-service duties.]**

## 4. Community-rule violations

Non-legal reports (NSFW content, spam, a broken remix) go to the same address
and are handled under the Terms. Our moderation actions are: remove the item,
hide the item from the showcase, or close the account. We tell the affected
user what happened and why, and we give them a route to dispute it
(**[SUPPORT EMAIL]**). **[LAWYER: UK Online Safety Act — regulated
user-to-user services must provide content-report and complaints procedures
and record-keeping regardless of size; see sources. Confirm this section
satisfies those duties.]**

## 5. Transparency

We publish a simple count of notices received and actions taken **[CADENCE —
suggest quarterly]**. A one-person service cannot publish a big-tech
transparency report, but an honest small one builds trust.

## 6. What is deliberately not promised

- No guarantee of pre-screening: user content is not reviewed before it goes
  public.
- No general monitoring obligation: we act on notices and on what we discover,
  not on omniscience.
- No commitment to host disputed content while disputes run, except where the
  law (DMCA counter-notice) requires it.
