# Bird-call specification sources

These are starting specifications for a ZEIM water-chirping bird call. Bird-call sound depends strongly on small changes in air-gap height, window size, chamber volume, water level, and sealing; print at least three tuning variants before treating a geometry as final.

## Sources

- AdamStag’s Chirping Bird Whistle is an established printable bird-whistle design and states that it prints cleanly in PLA or ABS and produces the same chirping effect in full and 75% scale versions.  
  https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:1155687
- Fabbaloo describes the chirping-bird-whistle concept, including an educational transparent version intended to show the internal chamber.  
  https://www.fabbaloo.com/news/design-of-the-week-chirping-bird-whistle
- A tinwhistle discussion documents a practical printed whistle-head geometry, including a 20 mm windway, 1.3 mm windway height, and 4 mm window length; ZEIM uses these as directional references, not exact copied dimensions.  
  https://www.chiffandfipple.com/t/3d-printed-whistle-head-strengthening-lower-octave/104823
- The Music Scientist explains that larger whistle cavities produce lower frequencies and provides the cavity-volume/frequency relationship.  
  https://www.themusicscientist.com/3d-printed-whistle-project-understanding-frequency-math-for-music-education/
- Can I 3D Print It notes that small changes around the air channel and blade strongly affect whether a printed whistle works, and recommends PETG for durability in some whistle designs.  
  https://cani3dprintit.com/whistle

## Calibration

The ZEIM dimensions are starting values, not guaranteed sound settings. Air-gap height, window width, chamber volume, water level, cap clearance, and print orientation should be tuned with the four provided test variants.
